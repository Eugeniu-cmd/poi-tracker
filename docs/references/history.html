POI Tracker — History Reference

v0.6 — 2026-09-09. The panel where any recorded pass can be deleted, not only the most recent one — scoped to one POI or the whole route, showing for each pass whether it has reached the sheet, and keeping a deleted pass visible only for as long as its removal is still queued. Labels are pared back: the scope control reads "POI" and "Route", and a row names its side alone. Reference device: Android tablet 10.6", 1000 × 600 px (2000 × 1200 physical at 2× density), landscape, full-screen.

Screen 1 · This POI

Decision map · Screen 1

Any pass can be deleted, not only the last

Every row carries its own Delete. This is the reason the panel exists: "Undo last" walks backwards from the end, and a mistake noticed three intersections later is not the last thing recorded.

Passes are entered at speed and errors surface late — often only when the counters stop matching what the shift actually drove. Forcing a correction to unwind every good pass recorded after the bad one would make people leave the error in rather than lose the work.

Each row says whether it was delivered

A pass reads "waiting" while it is still on the tablet and "sent" once it has reached the sheet, in the warning colour and the muted colour respectively.

The two states delete differently: one disappears locally, the other has to travel to the sheet as a removal. Showing the state before the tap is what makes the difference in outcome predictable instead of surprising.

The POI list stays live behind the panel

Another POI is chosen straight from the list already on screen beside the panel; the panel reloads in place for it, and History never closes.

Putting a second picker inside the panel would duplicate a control the screen already carries, and closing the panel to change POI would cost two taps for something done often — check one POI, correct it, move to the next.

The sub-header ranks its three facts

The POI or route you are actually looking at sits in an amber chip; the route and the city stay behind it as quiet context, and a total like the route's pass count gets a neutral chip of its own.

Three facts set in one weight force the eye to read all three to find the one that changes between screens. Amber already means "where I am" everywhere else in the app — the active POI row, the approach-side discs, the side chips — so reusing it here costs nothing to learn.

Hand-typed passes never appear

Only passes recorded on this tablet are listed. Numbers typed straight into the spreadsheet are absent, and the footer says so.

The app knows its own events; it does not know how a cell reached its value. Inventing rows for numbers it never recorded would promise a delete it cannot perform — those corrections belong in the sheet.

Screen 2 · Whole route

Decision map · Screen 2

One panel, two scopes

The same list switches between this POI and the whole route through a segmented control in its header, rather than living in two separate panels reached from different places.

The two views answer one question — "what did I record?" — at two zoom levels, and the row, its states and its Delete behave identically in both. Splitting them would duplicate every rule and force the passenger to guess which panel holds the pass they are hunting for.

Newest first

The most recent pass is at the top, and the list runs backwards in time from there.

Corrections are overwhelmingly about what just happened: a wrong side, a double tap at the last intersection. Putting the newest row where the eye lands makes the common case a glance rather than a scroll through the whole shift.

The POI is named only in this scope

Whole-route rows carry a POI column; the this-POI rows do not, because the line under the header already names it.

Repeating "POI 2" on six consecutive rows spends width on a constant and pushes the side and the delete control together. Across the route the POI is the column that actually varies, so that is where it earns its place.

Screen 3 · A deleted pass

Decision map · Screen 3

A deleted pass stays visible until the removal lands

The row is struck through, dimmed and labelled "deleted" while its removal is still queued. Once that removal has reached the sheet the row leaves the list for good.

A row still on screen is the evidence that something has not yet been delivered — it is the same promise the sync line makes, shown against the specific pass it concerns. Keeping every deletion for ever would turn that signal into decoration: after a full shift the struck-through rows outnumber the live ones and bury the passes that still matter.

Deleting a delivered pass is queued, not refused

The pass had already reached the sheet, so its removal becomes an event of its own, waiting in the queue like any pass. The note above the list counts what is pending.

Refusing would send the passenger to the spreadsheet for the one correction the app is best placed to make, and doing it silently on the tablet would leave the sheet holding a pass that no longer exists. Queueing a removal keeps both sides converging on the same answer through the one channel that already works offline.

How this differs from "Undo last"

"Undo last" only ever removes a pass that is still waiting on the tablet, and only from the end. Anything already sent is corrected here instead, which is why that button dims once the queue is empty.

Two controls with one job each stay predictable: undo is the quick fix for the tap you just made, History is the considered fix for anything older. Merging them would give a single button two very different consequences depending on state the passenger cannot see at a glance.
