Screen 1 · City list

Decision map · Screen 1

Cities are a list, not one address

Settings holds a list that grows: Dortmund, then Düsseldorf, then Vienna. Each entry carries its own connection details and its own routes rather than overwriting a single global field.

Every city is a separate Google tracker, so a single editable address would mean retyping it on every move and losing the previous city's routes and baseline the moment the work shifts.

The active city is marked twice

The row here carries the active highlight, and the same city name sits beside the route picker on the working screen.

A tap writes into one specific tracker. Showing the target only in Settings would mean the one screen where passes are actually recorded never says which city it is filling.

A city is never deleted automatically

Finishing a city and moving to the next one adds an entry; nothing is cleaned up on the way out. Removal is always an explicit act.

A finished city still holds its baseline and its history, and may still hold passes that never reached the sheet. Automatic tidying would throw those away silently.

Screen 2 · City — Düsseldorf

Decision map · Screen 2

Connection is one row, not three fields

The address and the key are entered once in the life of a city and then never read again, so they collapse into a single row that states its status and opens a screen of its own.

Ranking a screen by how often each thing is used beats ranking it by setup order. Three setup fields at the top push the routes — the reason the screen is opened at all — below a field nobody looks at twice.

Routes fill the screen

The route list takes the largest region and shows all six at once, each at full tap size, with adding a route at the end of the list.

Routes are the working content of a city: they are checked, corrected and added throughout a job. Content that changes often deserves the space, and seeing the whole set at once is what makes a missing or mistyped route obvious.

Deleting a city is explicit and low

The control sits at the bottom of the column in a muted destructive style, away from the routes and the connection row.

It is the only action here that can lose field work that never reached a spreadsheet. Distance and a quiet style are the cheapest guard against a mis-tap while scrolling.

Screen 3 · Connection — Düsseldorf

Decision map · Screen 3

The key and the URL live on the tablet

Both are entered here and saved to this device only. Neither ever appears in a project file, a prompt or a chat message.

The address plus its key is write access to the user's live tracker. Anything committed to the repository is effectively published, and a deployment address cannot be rotated as easily as a key.

"Script URL" and "Access key", not "Mailbox" and "Password"

The screen names each thing as the user meets it: the URL is literally the link Apps Script hands back after deployment, and the key is a secret this app invents for itself.

Mailbox is our internal word for the Apps Script and means nothing to the person typing; password would suggest the Google account credential and invite someone to paste it here. The documentation may keep saying mailbox — the interface may not.

Testing is a button, not an automatic act

"Test connection" sits beside Save and is pressed deliberately; the result appears as a status line under the buttons.

A test fired on every keystroke would hammer a live spreadsheet with half-typed addresses and turn a normal edit into a stream of failures. Pressing it is also the moment the user actually wants an answer.

Sending is automatic; the button only confirms

Passes go out on their own as soon as there is network, and the status line carries the time of the last successful send. "Send now" changes nothing about that — it is there for the moment before a shift ends, when the user wants to see it happen.

An offline-first app that waits to be told to sync loses data the day someone forgets. Automatic delivery removes that failure, but it leaves no moment of confirmation, and a driver parking up wants to know the day's work landed before closing the tablet. The button buys that certainty without becoming the mechanism.

"Load from sheet" is the bridge from hand entry

It reads what a route already holds in the sheet so the app can carry on from those numbers — the one way work done by hand becomes work the app can continue. It warns first whenever passes are still waiting to be sent, and it lives here rather than on the working screen because it overwrites local counts.

The sheet keeps seven totals per POI, not the passes that made them, so the app can continue from a hand-filled route but can never show the individual passes behind those totals — which is why History lists nothing for them. Loading before sending would silently discard the queue, because the tracker cannot know about passes that never reached it; and the working screen is tapped without looking, so no overwriting action belongs there.

Screen 4 · Route editor — R20890-028

Decision map · Screen 4

Passes per POI is shown, not editable

The figure sits in the form as a read-only line rather than being hidden away or offered as an input.

70 is a project-wide constant, so an editable field would invite a per-route value that nothing downstream honours. It still has to be visible, because it is the number every "N of 70" counter and every completion check is measured against.

Deleting a route with unsent passes is refused, and looks it

While the queue still holds passes for this route the control is inert. It keeps the same red destructive colour as "Delete city" and is only dimmed, with the caption underneath naming the count — "32 passes not yet sent".

Recolouring it grey would make it a different control: the eye stops recognising the one action on this screen that destroys work. Dimming leaves the identity intact and reads as "not now" rather than "not here", which is the truth — send the passes and the same red control becomes available. A warning that can be dismissed would destroy field work with one tap, and those passes exist nowhere else yet.
