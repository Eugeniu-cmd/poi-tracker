Screen 1 · Default — compass closed

Decision map · Screen 1

Two columns, always both

The POI list keeps its own column on the left while the discs own the right. Choosing a POI never covers the input surface, and the passenger can check "which POI am I on" without leaving the tap flow.

A full-screen list that slides over the buttons costs a second tap and a second glance every time — in a moving car that is where entries get lost.

The straight disc sits higher

The ↑ disc is raised above ← and →, so the three form an arc rather than a row.

A thumb reaching across a tablet travels an arc, not a straight line. Three discs on one line put the two easiest mis-taps directly next to each other.

Two colors, two roles

Maneuvers are dark solid discs; approach sides are amber solid discs. The colors never swap and never appear on anything that is not tappable.

CarPlay rule: two interactive roles must not share a color. If maneuver and side looked alike, every tap would need a read instead of a glance.

Glass on panels, solid on controls

The left panel, the route picker and the action bar are translucent and blurred. The discs are fully opaque.

Translucency is decoration and belongs to chrome. A control that lets the background through loses contrast in direct sunlight, which is exactly when the passenger is tapping.

The compass is real, not decoration

The needle points to the heading of the car, not of the tablet, and the ring stays fixed with North at the top. It is there so the passenger can name the approach side without guessing which way the car came in.

It reads GPS course while the car moves and the magnetometer when it stands still, because neither source works alone: GPS goes blind at a standstill and the magnetometer is bent by the vehicle body. The mount holds the tablet at a fixed but unknown angle to the car, so that offset is measured once on a straight road and subtracted from every reading afterwards.

The passenger taps

Sizes assume a passenger holding the tablet, not a driver reaching sideways (screens.md v0.4).

It does not shrink the targets: the car still moves, so moving-car tolerances stay. It only removes the constraint that everything must be reachable from the driver seat.

Screen 2 · Compass open on Left

Decision map · Screen 2

Compass, not a 12-button grid

Tapping a maneuver blooms four approach sides around that disc: N above, S below, W left, E right. The pass is written on the second tap.

Twelve one-tap buttons would be faster per pass but each is a quarter of the size, and a half-entered pass becomes impossible only when both halves are written together. The 3×4 grid stays a step-2 candidate.

The other maneuvers dim

The ↑ and → discs drop to 16 % opacity while the compass is open.

They are not valid targets in this state. Leaving them at full strength invites a tap that would either do nothing or silently switch maneuver.

"Cancel" appears only here

The escape button is absent in the default state and appears in the action bar only while the compass is open.

A permanent cancel button trains the eye to ignore it. Showing it exactly when there is something to cancel makes it findable without reading.

Auto-close after 12 seconds

If no side is tapped, the compass closes on its own, records nothing and says so.

An abandoned compass left open would attach the next stray tap to a maneuver chosen a minute ago at a different intersection.

Screen 3 · POI complete — controls locked

Decision map · Screen 3

A finished POI locks its controls

At 70 / 70 the three maneuver discs stay visible but dimmed and inert.

Hiding them would make the screen jump and leave the passenger wondering whether the app broke. Dimming keeps the layout still and makes the state readable at a glance.

The caption states the reason

A short line next to the discs reads "This POI is done: 70 of 70" instead of a generic disabled look.

A dead button with no explanation is indistinguishable from a bug. Naming the reason turns a dead end into a confirmation that the POI is done.

Undo stays available

"Undo last" is the one control that stays enabled on a finished POI.

Reaching 70 is exactly when a miscount hurts most, and a wrong 70th pass must be removable without opening the spreadsheet. Undo removes a whole pass, never half of one.
